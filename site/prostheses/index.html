Adding a Neural Prosthetic to a Language Model Without Harming It

A 7.04 MB frozen-host calculator and symbolic algebra sidecar lifts Qwen3.5-4B arithmetic from 42.2% to 92.2% and symbolic CAS from 23.3% to 100.0% with zero harm across 4,457 non-math items (−0.09 pp, p = 0.69). On a 1.22 GB quantized host, it beats the unquantized 8.41 GB model on math and heals general benchmarks (+1.66 pp, p = 2.4 × 10−5).

Tool fine-tuning buys capability by rewriting the base model

Language models fail reliably on multi-digit arithmetic, exact rational fractions, modular inverses, and symbolic algebra. The standard remedy — fine-tuning the model’s weights to emit calculator tags or Python programs [1] [2] [3] [4] — raises math scores but silently edits every layer of the base network. Published tool papers rarely measure paired degradation on general benchmarks against the exact parent checkpoint, or they rely on aggregate averages where replay on code or QA masks general-knowledge forgetting and instruction-following collapse.

We ask a stricter question: can a language model gain exact numeric arithmetic and symbolic computer algebra as a Pareto improvement — dramatically better on calculation, statistically indistinguishable from the untouched host on general non-math benchmarks, and runnable on top of an already-quantized frozen model at nearly native speed?

Our answer builds on neuron-homology maps [5] and cross-scale circuit grafting [6]. Rather than updating the host’s weights, we attach a 7.04 MB (3.42M-parameter, 0.081% of the 4.21B host) parallel sidecar anchored to the host’s own 6,857 mapped calculation neurons (2.3% of its MLP neurons), an activation-only intent gate read from the sidecar’s private stream, and an in-process exact arithmetic and SymPy co-processor. Every weight of the host remains bit-for-bit frozen, verified by checksum.

A parallel sidecar over the host’s calculation neurons separates calculation intent from general text

At each layer l of Qwen3.5-4B [7], the host computes its normal residual update untouched. In parallel, the prosthesis reads the host’s clean residual stream xclean = x − δH through a trained rank-8 read adapter, runs the frozen slice of the host’s own calculation neurons with learned per-neuron gain α and soft-threshold τ, accumulates its internal state in a private sidecar stream sD, and writes back through a zero-initialized rank-8 write adapter:

ul = RMSNorml( xclean + (xclean − μl) UA,lVA,lT + sD )
vl = soft_shrink(fcalc,l(ul) − ml; τl) ⊙ αl · Wdown,calc,l,   sD ← sD + vl
ΔH,l = βl vl + vl UB,lVB,lT,   δH ← δH + ΔH,l
  • Zero extra neuron storage. On both 16-bit and quantized hosts, the 6,857 calculation neurons are sliced directly from the host’s own frozen MLPs. Only the small rank-8 adapters, per-neuron gains/thresholds, and intent gate are stored: 7.04 MB total in FP16 (0.58% of a 1.22 GB quantized model).
  • Split activation intent and syntactic verification. A compact classifier reads multi-stage taps of the private sidecar stream sD (layers 11, 21, 31) plus detached xclean. Whenever the model emits an equals sign (“=”), the co-processor fires only if both the activation gate signals genuine calculation intent (blocking verbatim copy traps with zero string heuristics) and a strict AST/SymPy parser confirms the left-hand side is an evaluable numeric or CAS expression (blocking symbolic equations like x2 − 4 = 0 and trivial identities).
  • On-policy reasoning and general-text KL anchoring. Training minimizes KL divergence to the intact host on held-out general prose, KL divergence to the host’s own self-generated GSM8K and MATH rollouts (masking computed result digits so reasoning length and style stay untouched), and cross-entropy on verified calculator injections and hard negatives.

Three controlled waves turn a 46-point arithmetic lift with regressions into a strict Pareto improvement

Evaluating every variant on the exact same frozen 780-problem math, symbolic CAS, and copy-control split — 180 twelve-family exact arithmetic problems, 150 multi-step word problems with large numbers, 200 GSM8K [8], 150 MATH [9], 60 held-out symbolic CAS problems, and 40 verbatim copy traps — isolates why each design choice is necessary (Figure 3).

  • Wave 1 (off-policy traces vs. matched LoRA). Anchoring the sidecar to the calculation neurons cuts general-text KL 2.75× relative to a parameter-matched MLP LoRA [10] (0.00185 vs. 0.00508 nats) while lifting exact arithmetic from 42.2% to 82.2% (and 87.8% with a zero-text-parsing activation read bus). However, training on terse off-policy traces shrank reasoning length fourfold (404 → 103 tokens on GSM8K; 732 → 195 on MATH), dropping GSM8K from 92.0% to 81.5% and MATH from 72.0% to 59.3% (and 40.0% when plain “=” fired on symbolic equations).
  • Wave 2 (on-policy traces + split intent gate). Replacing terse traces with the intact host’s own verified GSM8K and MATH rollouts plus an on-policy reasoning KL anchor restored native reasoning length (406 tokens on GSM8K; 719 on MATH) and eliminated every regression simultaneously. The gated native prosthesis strictly beat or matched the intact 4B on all six tests: exact arithmetic 84.4%, word arithmetic 95.3%, GSM8K 93.0%, MATH 75.3%, copy control 100.0% (zero false calls), HellaSwag 73.0% vs. 72.6%, and pooled 680-problem math 69.4% → 87.4% (p = 3.2 × 10−24) at 0.00176 KL. Even the strict zero-text-parsing variant recovered MATH from 40.0% to 71.3% and GSM8K from 75.5% to 91.0%.
  • Wave 3 (symbolic SymPy CAS + full 4,457-item standard audit). Adding named state-tracking traces for multi-step families plus passive zero-variable SymPy evaluation and six explicit CAS primitives (prime factorization, modular inverse, factoring, expansion, solving, simplification) lifted exact arithmetic to 92.2%, held-out symbolic CAS from 23.3% to 100.0% (60/60), and pooled 740-problem math+CAS from 65.7% to 88.0% (+187/−22, p = 8.6 × 10−34), while achieving our lowest general-text KL of any trained arm (0.00164 nats) and zero significant loss across all 4,457 standard non-math items (62.84% → 62.76%, −0.09 pp, p = 0.69).

On a 1.22 GB quantized host, the 7.04 MB prosthesis beats the unquantized 8.41 GB model and heals non-math benchmarks

Quantizing Qwen3.5-4B to 2.32 bits per weight (1.22 GB via LookAtMyQuant 2 [11]) shrinks the model 6.8× but drops 740-problem math+CAS from 65.7% to 63.6%, introduces 3 verbatim copy errors (92.5%), raises KL against the 16-bit model to 0.306 nats, and lowers pooled 4,457-item standard accuracy from 62.84% to 53.31%.

Attaching our 7.04 MB prosthesis to the frozen 1.22 GB quantized weights (1.229 GB total, slicing the 6,857 calculation neurons directly from the quantized host) produces two striking results (Figure 4):

  • Zero-shot transfer with zero gradient steps already beats 16-bit intact. Plugging Wave 2’s BF16-trained prosthesis straight onto the frozen 1.22 GB model and recentering only the activation means and standard deviations jumps 680-problem pooled math from 66.2% to 73.7% (p = 4.0 × 10−6 vs. quantized intact; p = 0.01 above the 6.8× larger 16-bit host’s 69.4%).
  • BF16-anchored adaptation beats 16-bit math by 18.2–20.4 points and heals general capability. Training the 7.04 MB prosthesis for 1,000 steps on the frozen 1.22 GB host anchored to 16-bit KL lifts pooled 740-problem math+CAS to 83.9% in v2 (86.1% in v1; p = 6.1 × 10−20 and 4.3 × 10−24 over unquantized 8.41 GB intact), restores verbatim copying to 100.0%, cuts quantization KL by 16.7% (0.306 → 0.255 nats), and produces a statistically significant +1.66 pp (+74 net items, p = 2.4 × 10−5) recovery across the 4,457 standard non-math items: HumanEval +7.3 pp (p = 0.043), HellaSwag +5.0 pp (p = 4.2 × 10−7), CommonsenseQA +1.7 pp (p = 0.0099), ARC-Challenge +1.5 pp (p = 0.069), and MMLU flat (−0.14 pp, p = 0.92).

Four published calculator and math-tool fine-tunes all churn 15%–34% of standard answers and collapse to 0% on copy controls

To test whether general-benchmark preservation is genuinely unusual in practice, we evaluated four published ungated calculator and math-tool model pairs across all 5,237 items: the exact same 4,457-item standard suite — MMLU [12] (1,400 stratified), ARC-Challenge [13] (1,172), HellaSwag [14] (500), CommonsenseQA [15] (1,221), and HumanEval [16] (164, with SentencePiece-safe joint-sequence decoding so base indentation is preserved) — plus 32-passage forward prose KL (Table 1, Figure 1) and the exact same 780-item Math, Symbolic CAS, and Copy Control suite running each tool model’s official prompt and live SymPy/Python interpreter against its untooled parent baseline (Table 2). For ToRA-Code-7B [3], we also restored its true training RoPE base (rope_theta = 10,000, hardcoded in its original transformers 4.31 / vLLM 0.1.4 stack rather than the unused 1,000,000 left in CodeLlama’s config), lifting its GSM8K from 33% to 68% and MMLU from 31.3% to 36.9%.

Every published full-weight fine-tune exhibits severe behavioral drift, in two distinct regimes. First, direct calculator and math-specialist fine-tunes collapse general benchmarks outright: Calcformer-FLAN-XL [2] (the closest published SymPy calculator-tag analog) drops −15.02 pp across 4,293 items (p = 1.1 × 10−74), and Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct [7] drops −20.78 pp across 4,457 items (p = 2.8 × 10−136, KL = 2.210 nats, 1,350× ours). Second, Python-TIR models trained on raw base checkpoints look milder in pooled multiple-choice averages yet still rewrite the base model: OpenMath-Mistral-7B [4] loses significant MMLU (−2.43 pp, p = 0.03) with 15.0% churn and 138× higher KL (0.226 nats), while ToRA-Code-7B [3] finishes net-positive on pooled QA (+1.68 pp, p = 0.0095) over its Python-only base yet still drops HellaSwag (−3.2 pp, p = 0.036), flips 18.3% of answers, and drifts 232× in prose KL (0.380 nats). Most strikingly, all four published tool models collapse from 62.5%–100% down to 0.0% (0/40) on Copy Control (invoking Python/SymPy or solving arithmetic inside lines explicitly marked “copy verbatim”), whereas our BF16 prosthesis scores 100.0% (40/40). On the 740-problem Math + CAS benchmark, our 4B prosthesis reaches 88.0% (100.0% on Symbolic CAS), outscoring both 7B Python-TIR models — OpenMath-Mistral-7B (80.3%) and ToRA-Code-7B (77.0%) — and landing within 2.8 pp of Qwen2.5-Math-7B (90.8%), with zero significant drop on any general benchmark (MMLU p = 0.54, non-STEM MMLU −0.34 pp p = 0.45, 1.3% churn).

Inference speed: eliminating Python hook overhead recovers near-baseline throughput, while calculator injection speeds up hard math up to 8.0×

Full-weight fine-tunes modify weights in place and therefore run at ~100% of their parent’s per-token speed on non-math queries (with any wall-clock difference on generative benchmarks like HumanEval coming from shorter or longer completions). By contrast, a frozen-host sidecar adds a small parallel computation on every layer — an important practical cost whenever a host is kept frozen or quantized (where adapters cannot be merged into low-bit trellis weights).

To measure real inference overhead without cold-CUDA order bias, we ran a warm, counterbalanced A-B-B-A timing audit with full-shape untimed warmup and GPU synchronization across all five standard benchmarks plus fixed-length forced decode (batch sizes 1 and 16, 128 tokens) on H100 (Figure 5, Table 3; baseline pass-1 vs. pass-2 MC drift ≤ 0.3%). Our Wave 1–3 research harness attached 169 eager Python forward hooks per token with redundant FP32 casts, holding HumanEval decode to 77.0% of baseline. Replacing those hooks with a zero-hook, pure-BF16 inline wrapper over the exact same checkpoint (98.81% top-1 logit agreement) lifts speed to 91.1% on 4,293 MC+MMLU items, 90.4% wall-clock (88.8% tok/s) on HumanEval, and 75.2% / 79.1% on fixed-length B=1 / B=16 decode (vs. 96.7% MC / 92.2% B=16 for unmerged parameter-matched MLP LoRA and 17.0% MC for a 325-token tool-schema system prompt without prefix caching). Notably, even among published full-weight fine-tunes, ToRA-Code-7B’s addition of a single padding token (resizing the LM head from 32,000 to an unaligned 32,001) drops MC prefill to 93.4% of CodeLlama-7B-Python while keeping decode at 100.1%. On hard arithmetic and symbolic CAS, injecting exact results cuts token length 5×–10× (746 → 69 tokens on CAS), making our prosthesis 1.2× faster on exact arithmetic and 8.0× faster on symbolic CAS end-to-end (3.5× faster on the 1.22 GB host) even before hook removal.

The complete study cost $78 of GPU time

All training, evaluation, external-pair audits, and speed benchmarks ran on rented Modal H100 GPUs: $11.05 for Wave 1 (4 arms + baseline), $11.71 for Wave 2 (on-policy rollout prep + 2 synthesis arms), $21.83 for Wave 3 (v1 + v2 symbolic CAS, 1.22 GB quantized rescue, and full 4,457-item standard audits), $6.52 for the external standard-benchmark audit (including SentencePiece HumanEval and ToRA RoPE refresh), $12.49 for the 780-item external Math, CAS, and Copy Control benchmark across all four published tool runtimes, and $14.69 for the warm counterbalanced speed audit.

Limitations

  • Single host family. All prostheses were trained on Qwen3.5-4B (16-bit BF16 and 2.32-bit quantized). Generalizing the same mapped-neuron sidecar to other model families and larger scales remains open.
  • Single seeds and Pareto trade-off on word arithmetic. Each arm is a single training run; p-values reflect paired item variation rather than seed variance. Adding symbolic CAS in Wave 3 v2 reached 92.2% exact arithmetic and 100.0% CAS at 0.00164 KL, while Wave 2 held a lead on multi-step word arithmetic (95.3% vs. 90.0%) and MATH (75.3% vs. 70.7%), leaving a minor batch-mix trade-off.
  • Untooled base-model denominators vary across published pairs. Raw base checkpoints (Mistral-7B, CodeLlama-7B-Python) start much lower on math than Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct (0%–15% vs. 66%–70% pooled), so Table 2 reports raw parent and tool accuracy rather than a ratio, which a near-zero parent score inflates.
  • Simulated BF16 dequantization on H100 for the quantized host. The 1.22 GB quantized weights were decoded faithfully from the real 2.32-bit packed artifact into PyTorch BF16 for GPU training and benchmarking; fusing the 7.04 MB sidecar into a native packed Apple Silicon MLX or CUDA trellis kernel is the next deployment step.
